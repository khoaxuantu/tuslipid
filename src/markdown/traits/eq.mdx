## Mild self-awareness

I'm aware of my emotions in a general sense - I know when I'm stressed, upset, or happy - but the
deeper layer of self-understanding is still developing. I may find it hard to explain why I feel a
certain way, or notice that my reactions sometimes surprise even myself.

### My blind spot at this level

The most significant blind spot is usually the connection between past experiences and present
reactions. Many of my emotional responses are rooted in patterns that formed long before I were
aware of them - and without reflection, those patterns just keep repeating. I may find myself having
the same arguments, the same frustrations, or the same self-doubts in different situations without
quite knowing why.

### How others experience me

Others may sometimes fiind me a little hard to read, or feel like they're getting a different
version of me depending on my mood. In calm, comfortable situations I'm probably warm and engaged.
In more stressful moments, people around me may not know quite what to expect, which can make it
harder for them to connect with me or offer the right kind of support.

## Mild self-regulation

Strong emotions are frequently getting ahead of my ability to manage them, and that gap is creating
real costs - in my relationships, in how I'm perceived at work, and in how I feel about myself
afterward. This isn't about willpower or trying harder. It means the specific skills of emotional
regulation haven't been fully built yet, and those skills can be learned.

### My trigger profile

At this level, triggers tend to be broad rather than specific - meaning a lot of different
situations can set off a strong emotional reaction. Fealling criticized, feeling overlooked, feeling
out of control, or simply being tired and stretched can all push me past my threshold relatively
quickly.

### My regulation toolkit

Right now my toolkit is limited, and the strategies I do have may not kick in quickly enough to
make a difference in the heat of the moment. That's not a personal failing, it just means the tools
haven't been practiced enough to become automatic. A skill only helps when it's deeply enough
ingrained that I can access it under pressure, without having to think too hard about it.

### My recovery pattern

Recovery is currently slow and effortful. After an emotionally charged event, I may stay activated
for hours - replaying the situation, feeling ashamed, or struggling to switch my focus to something
else. This prolonged recovery is draining and can affect my performance and my relationships long
after the original event has passed. Building even one simple recovery habit can start to shorten
that window.

## Mild motivation

My motivation is present, but it's fragile and inconsistent. I may feel driven in short bursts -
especially when something feels urgent or personally relevant - but sustaining that effort over time
is genuinely hard. Procrastination, self-doubt, and the gap between knowing what I should do and
actually doing it are familiar companions. This is more about unclear direction than lack of
character.

### My motivation style

At this level, motivation tends to be reactive rather than proactive - it shows up in response to
pressure or urgency rather than from a clear internal pull. Goals may feel vague, or the connection
between daily effort and meaningful outcomes may be hard to see. This makes it very difficult to
stay motivated through the inevitable slow or difficult phases of any worthwhile pursuit.

### What's fueling me - and what's draining me

- What tends to fuel me:
  - Situations where the stakes feel personally real and immediate.
  - Tasks that are clear and achievable.
  - The presence of someone who believes in me.
  - A small win that proves progress is possible.
- What tends to drain me:
  - Goals that feel too big or too abstract.
  - The absence of feedback or visible results.
  - Comparison with others.
  - Past experiences of stating something and not ending good.

## Moderate empathy

I genuinely care about the people around me and can connect emotionally in most everyday situations.
Where my empathy becomes less reliable is in moments of stress, in conversations with people who
are different from me, or when someone's emotional signals are subtle rather than obvious.

### How I show up in others' difficult moments

In most situations, I hope to respond with warm and care. Where it gets harder is when the emotional
need in front of me is intense, ongoing, or unclear. I may find myself shifting toward problem-solving
mode - not because I don't care, but because sitting in someone's pain without being able to fix it
feels uncomfortable.

Despite saying problem-solving mode, I still tends to prioritize others' preferences first. If they
do not want to share the problems with me, I will never try to interfere.

### My empathy gaps

My gaps tend to emerge in three situations:

- When I'm under pressure and have less bandwith to attend to others.
- When the person's reaction seems out of propotion to me.
- When their experience is very different from anything I've personally been through.

In each case, the gap isn't indifference - it's a lack of the attunement skills needed to bridge
the distance.
